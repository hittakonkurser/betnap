How to read a racecard

A racecard packs a great deal into very little space, and most of it is abbreviated. Once you know what the columns mean it reads quickly.

The race header

Above the runners you get the race itself: the off time and course, the race name, its class or grade, the distance in miles and furlongs, the going, the number of runners and the prize money. A furlong is an eighth of a mile, so a race described as 1m 4f is a mile and a half.

The going describes the ground, running from firm through good to heavy. Many horses have a clear preference, which is why it is one of the first things a tipster mentions.

Form figures

The string of characters before a horse's name is its recent form, with the most recent run on the right. This is the part that confuses newcomers most often.

SymbolMeaning
1–9Finishing position
0Finished outside the first nine
P or PUPulled up
FFell
U or URUnseated rider
B or BDBrought down by another horse
RRefused
–Separates one season from the next
/A longer absence, often a missed season

So 4/2131 reads as a horse that ran once, was off for a long spell, then finished second, first, third and most recently won. The gap matters: a horse returning from a year away is a different proposition from one in regular work.

The runner's line

ItemWhat it tells you
Saddlecloth numberIdentifies the horse during the race
Draw, in bracketsStarting stall on the flat. On some courses and distances an inside or outside draw is a real advantage
AgeYears old, which matters in races open to several age groups
WeightStones and pounds, written like 9-07. In a handicap the better horses carry more
Jockey and trainerOften with the claim an apprentice jockey takes off the weight
OROfficial rating, the handicapper's assessment of ability

Headgear

A letter after the horse's name means it is wearing something intended to help it concentrate. First-time headgear is worth noticing, because it usually means the trainer thinks something needs fixing.

LetterHeadgear
bBlinkers
vVisor
pCheekpieces
hHood
tTongue tie
eEye shield or eye cover
